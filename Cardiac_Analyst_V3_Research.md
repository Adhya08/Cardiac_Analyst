# Cardiac Analyst V3
## Product, evidence and research strategy
**22 November 2026 IST · Repository snapshot 551ce6dc45268034ca1ba2a003a929e48408af07**

## A. Executive summary

Build a **visit-preparation workspace for adults in India who already measure blood pressure at home and have a scheduled follow-up**. The first job is simple: turn scattered readings and reports into a concise, traceable visit packet in English or Hindi. Keep the 12-lead ECG pipeline as a research workspace with a separate intended use. Keep hospital discovery out of the product's primary promise while branch accuracy and failure handling remain unverified.

Start with guided BP logging, a measurement-quality timeline, a private document inbox, a one-page visit packet and bilingual accessibility. The current app already does parts of logging, charting, ECG viewing and print. V3 should connect those pieces around an appointment and remove misleading medical claims. There is evidence for structured BP monitoring integrated with care, and evidence that patients value access to results. There is **no direct evidence yet** that the proposed Indian audience will switch tools, clinicians will use the packet, or users will pay.

Treat the old production audit as historical evidence. The latest source fixes several specific findings and introduces optical ECG ingestion with new validity risks. Live health still reports unloaded vitals ML and unavailable Sarvam. Neither a green health endpoint nor the published waveform benchmark validates the current production experience.

## C. Target user research

| Segment | Workflow, existing tools and reason to return | Demonstrated evidence and local hypothesis | Barriers, harms and adoption test |
|---|---|---|---|
| Adults with home cuffs | Record prescribed readings, review a week before follow-up, bring paper, cuff memory or spreadsheet | HOME BP (2021) and TASMINH4 (2018) support clinician-integrated self-monitoring interventions. Neither isolates a standalone logging app. Indian users switching from WhatsApp/paper is a hypothesis. | Repeated entry, inconsistent timing, mixed units, low connectivity and anxiety. Test guided repeated readings against their usual method and ask whether they would bring the output to a real visit. |
| People managing known cardiac disease | Collect existing findings and medication lists, locate last result at review, share selected records | Portal and proxy studies support retrieval/access problems in their studied settings. Cross-provider fragmentation and report formats in this cohort require fieldwork. | Fabricated medical history, wrong person in shared-phone homes and oversharing. Test source retrieval, identity selection and omission of sensitive documents. |
| People receiving reports | Read reports at irregular intervals, search terminology, prepare questions | Steitz et al. (2023) found strong preference for immediate access across surveyed portal users, with higher worry among those seeing abnormal results. The setting does not represent all Indian patients. Radiology-summary trials offer indirect evidence for comprehension, not cardiac safety. | Language, scan quality and false reassurance. Compare source-linked glossary against an unannotated report through teach-back. |
| Caregivers | Organize visits and readings for relatives, help with sharing or access | US hospital proxy research (2020) documents insecure password sharing and limited granularity. India-specific device sharing and delegation patterns need interviews. | Coercion, exposure on shared devices, mistaken upload and stale access. Test invite, scoped view and revoke with separate accounts and explicit permission. |
| Cardiologists / primary-care reviewers | Triage a limited appointment's information, interpret denominators and inspect originals | PGHD literature identifies workflow fit and provenance as issues. Evidence for the proposed packet's time savings is absent. | Extra portal, alarm burden and ambiguous inputs. Time a one-page artifact versus a raw spreadsheet. Do not require an account for the first clinician task. |
| Terminology seekers | Look up one phrase, ask a follow-up question, leave until next report | Report-explanation evidence points to a comprehension question worth testing. No evidence of high-frequency retention for one-off definitions. | False personalization, language mistranslation and urgency omissions. Test comprehension and uncertainty recognition without collecting documents. |

The focused cohort is adults with an existing clinician-directed home BP plan, using a cuff, who bring information to follow-up. Recruit both English- and Hindi-preferring users, including Android-first and shared-device users. This is a practical first cohort because the trigger and output can be observed within one visit cycle. Use caregivers as a research subgroup, not as the primary buyer. Avoid prescribing a universal measurement cadence; keep the patient's clinician-defined schedule visible.

## F. AI/ML research agenda

| Rank | Question, data and experiment | Metrics and evidence needed |
|---|---|---|
| R1 | **Can the current ResNet generalize?** Freeze full training lineage, thresholds and artifact. Compare a small CNN and published 1D ResNet/xResNet under matching preprocessing, tuning on validation only. Use PTB-XL and a non-overlapping compatible CPSC/Chapman subset where labels harmonize. Do not claim equivalence across incompatible endpoints; the 2021 challenge collection includes PTB-XL, so explicitly exclude it from an external set. | Per-class AUPRC, AUROC, recall at fixed precision, F1, patient-cluster intervals, calibration and CPU latency. Report prevalence, site, subgroup sizes and exclusions. Distinguish dataset reproduction from clinical validation. |
| R2 | **Does signal quality predict unreliable output?** Use PTB-XL signal metadata and generated artifact corruptions, then independent labelled noise/device examples. Compare flatline, clipping, missing lead, drift and sampling rules vs lightweight quality classifier. Fit per-class calibration on development data and add abstention. | Accept/reject accuracy, coverage-risk curves, Brier score/reliability and performance by noise/device. Stop if performance gain comes primarily from rejecting underrepresented groups. Temperature scaling is established; transfer calibration is uncertain. |
| R3 | **Does optical reconstruction preserve voltage, timing and diagnostic performance?** Compare the current grid extraction with a segmentation baseline using the 2024 Challenge and paired image–waveform data. Test paper speeds, gain, cropped and rotated scans, differing lead layouts and acquisition timing. Do not silently stretch short lead segments into fully simultaneous 10-second records. | Signal fidelity, timing and amplitude errors, lead identity, waveform-vs-image class degradation and failure/abstention rates. Require intended layouts with real-device images independently reviewed. |
| R4 | **Can users verify explanations?** Compare deterministic glossary/templates with retrieval plus structured generated outputs on synthetic and permitted public contexts, with independently reviewed Hindi and English. Include omitted findings, negation, wrong dates, source switching, missing pages and impossible questions. | Claim-level support, evidence location accuracy, omissions, unsafe advice, useful abstention, patient teach-back, reviewer time, token/latency cost. Any serious unsupported medical claim blocks pilot use. |
| R5 | **Are change displays and attributions reliable?** Start with audited paired display; evaluate automated morphology change against longitudinal annotations where available. Compare occlusion and attribution stability under lead/sampling/noise perturbations and parameter randomization. Optional multifusion only if paired data and a clear endpoint exist. | Paired-change adjudication, missing data rates, explanation agreement/stability and added reviewer value. Do not present raw attribution heatmaps as proof. Longitudinal dataset availability is unresolved. |

Repository-reported macro AUROC 0.9265, AUPRC 0.8196 and macro F1 0.752 apply to its held-out waveform evaluation. The report lists 110 misses among 550 MI positives and 116 among 262 HYP positives. Those results do not justify acute diagnosis or normal clearance. NICE CG95 states that a normal resting ECG cannot exclude ACS. Use deterministic displays and remove unsupported probability/clinical-action copy. UCI risk-model training uses diagnostic presence, not future events. A separate prospective risk model project would need a suitable time-to-event cohort, clear endpoint, population transportability and validated input measurement; skip it for V3. [Model results](https://github.com/Adhya08/Cardiac_Analyst/blob/551ce6dc45268034ca1ba2a003a929e48408af07/backend/models/EVALUATION_REPORT.md) · [UCI](https://archive.ics.uci.edu/dataset/45/heart+disease) · [Framingham, 2008](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18212285/) · [NICE CG95 recommendations](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/cg95/chapter/recommendations)

Check dataset release licenses, attribution and distribution restrictions per dataset and derivative. PTB-XL is openly listed by PhysioNet; Challenge datasets and their component terms must be reviewed individually before redistribution or commercial use. No new training data was downloaded and no licenses for derived models were legally assessed. Keep server-side ONNX initially for repeatability, centralized rollback and stable CPU behavior. Prototype client-side ONNX only after testing mobile memory, download time, parity and resource budgets; browser inference reduces upload needs when used without server persistence, while browser caching and shared-device risks still apply.

## G. Technical feasibility and architecture impact

Keep React and FastAPI. Add a visit packet operation over selected vitals and document metadata. Extend existing chart projections with source, count, units and report period. ## K. Recommended positioning

**“Bring a clear, shareable record of your home BP readings and reports to your next appointment.”** The key comparison is with the patient's current workflow, whether that’s a paper notebook, WhatsApp photos, an existing app or an institutional portal. Apple Health is a strong device ecosystem. Cardiac Analyst has a plausible reason to be chosen by someone using a basic cuff, handling mixed-provider reports, and seeking a bilingual, auditable appointment packet. That advantage needs direct testing.

Recurring usage should follow the existing clinician-directed plan. Offer prompt control, neutral trends and completion feedback. Avoid streaks, anxiety-inducing risk changes and compulsive rechecking. Make original-source access, edit history, actual data coverage and sharing scope visible. One-time export, a chat box and attractive charts are easy to copy. A consistent, independently evaluated workflow that reduces information-finding time, and a reproducible clinical research pipeline for 12-lead data, offer stronger differentiation. There is no paid LLM dependency in the proposed primary journey.

**Primary journey:** enter or review a planned BP session → verify the timeline and missing data → attach selected original reports → preview a one-page packet → download or share only selected material → ask the clinician whether it improved the visit. Avoid emergency triage, diagnostic prediction, risk-score marketing and broad provider connectivity as reasons to sign up.

## M. Validation and usability plan

**Recruitment.** Begin with 12–16 adults with a recent home-BP follow-up, including English/Hindi and Android/shared-device users, 4–6 caregivers and 5–8 clinicians (primary care plus cardiology). Recruit with permission through multiple channels to avoid convenience-sample bias. Include limited digital literacy, intermittent bandwidth and accessibility needs. These are proposed formative sample sizes, not inferential samples.

**Interviews.** Ask participants to walk through their last visit and reconstruct where information lived, how they recorded it, what they took, what was abandoned, what the clinician requested, whether they reuse an app and how they handle shared phones. Ask clinicians which readings they ignore, what information they need to verify, and how much review time they can allocate. Ask caregivers what access they need and what they would expect revocation to do. Ask about actual past behavior before pitching a solution. Never request upload of real records in this research phase.

**Tasks and comparisons.** Use synthetic cases with paired BP entries, missing days, an amended reading, bilingual dates and a mixed report set. Compare against the participant's familiar workflow and a relevant direct alternative such as SmartBP or Eka using dummy materials; counterbalance order and use matched scenarios. Observe entry and correction, reading denominator recognition, original report retrieval, redaction, offline recovery, PDF export, revoked link and identification of unknown results. Clinicians see randomized packet and raw-log versions, locate the requested reading and its source, and rate workload and usefulness. Secondary tests compare glossary vs unannotated text, share/revoke vs a simulated shared login, and reminder discovery and opt-out.

| Candidate | Proposed advance decision |
|---|---|
| BP log and visit timeline | At least 80% complete a planned paired session and recover from a bad entry without assistance, with no persistent unit/date/patient mismatches; compare time and error against baseline. |
| Visit packet | Median clinician time to find and verify three predefined facts improves by at least 25% against matched raw logs, with no increase in wrong interpretations. At least 70% of target users independently export a correctly scoped packet during formative tests. |
| Document inbox | At least 80% can locate the correct source page. Require repeated evidence of independently solving a real workflow gap across interview and prototype; defer if paper/manual attachment is sufficient. |
| Bilingual explanation | Test teach-back in each language. Require improved factual comprehension and no serious unsupported, missed or wrongly reassuring statements on the adjudicated safety test set. |
| Measurement reminders and delegation | Users must show prior missed planned sessions or a recurrent shared-access need. At least 90% can disable reminders and revoke access on task. Revocation and all forbidden data access must pass automated checks. |
| Device imports and deeper integrations | Prioritize a device or file source only after recurring duplicate-entry friction clusters around it. Require round-trip schema checks, deduplication and documented failure handling. |
| Retention | Use appointment-cycle retention, not daily opens. In a permissioned pilot, track first completed BP session, first correct packet, use at a real follow-up and return among people actually eligible for a second cycle. Report numerators, denominators and reasons for drop-off. |

Record selected event counts and durations without raw text, files, readings, coordinates or cross-site tracking. Use randomly generated participant IDs, consent and withdrawal, small-cell suppression by language, limited retention and simple access control. Obtain organizational/ethics approval before a patient pilot when applicable, with a plan for urgent disclosures during research. Start with synthetic materials and no telemetry on health values. Report sessions with missing or inaccessible data. Thresholds above are proposed product go/no-go gates, not clinical validation or statistical significance.

## N. Uncertainties and unanswered questions

- The exact live release, authentic user journeys, storage region, clinical policy, provider account pricing and permissions remain unverified. The observed health checks establish only what those endpoints report.
- App Store complaints are selective and market-dependent. No representative complaint sample, paid competitor accounts, device testing or exact India pricing for several tiers was available. Competitor UX analysis reflects documented workflows and available reviews.
- No direct interviews, business-model tests, retention measurements or comparative usability study were conducted. Indian demand, willingness to pay and the best buyer remain hypotheses.
- Privacy/legal requirements, ABDM integration, dataset derivatives and medical-device applicability depend on specific use and need specialist verification before release. No external cohort was downloaded and no model was retrained.
- The optical feature has no independently demonstrated trace fidelity. Mixed-format clinical reports, accurate Hindi comprehension and longitudinal ECG evaluation lack validated datasets for this proposed use.
- The source of the hospital coordinates seen in the screenshot was not established. Do not assume the source-level popup and SOS changes resolved location correctness in production.
